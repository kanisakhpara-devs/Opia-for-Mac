Layers, text and a brush.

A picture editor in the spirit of Photoshop. Stack layers with blend modes, cut things out, paint, and add text and shapes that stay editable.

  • Selections, magic wand, free transform
  • Levels, curves, hue and saturation, live

Cut it together. Export an MP4.

Put clips, pictures, titles and music on a timeline. Trim, split, snap, fade and crossfade, then export a film that plays anywhere.

  • Any number of video and audio tracks
  • Titles, crossfades, picture-in-picture

Songs, even if you don't read music.

Riffs for drums, bass, chords and melody that always fit together. Drag in your own sounds, record, cut and add effects when you want more.

Also in the box

Small things that make it nice to use.

  • Beginner and Advanced. Start with the essentials in bigger controls; switch on every panel when you're ready.
  • Four themes. Opia Dark, Midnight Teal, Porcelain and Lilac Haze.
  • English and Spanish. Change language any time, while the app is running.
  • Keyboard friendly. Every button reachable with Tab, menus on F10, shortcuts you can change.
  • Trackpad mode. Two fingers pan, a pinch zooms, sliders get bigger.
  • Plug-ins. Add your own commands in Python.

Private by design

Your files never leave your desk.

Opia has no account and no cloud. Photos, songs, videos and documents stay in your own folders, and the originals are never changed. It only goes online for map tiles, for FTP uploads you start yourself, and to check for updates once you've allowed it.

FAQ

Questions, answered honestly.

Is it free?

Yes. There is nothing to pay and no account to make. The project hasn't chosen an open-source licence yet, so for now the code is shared to look at and run, not to reuse.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything runs on your computer. Only the map in Lens needs the internet, to fetch map tiles.

Which computers does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 and later) and Macs with Intel processors; there's a download for each. It's an alpha: every build is opened and checked on GitHub's Macs, but it hasn't been tried on many real ones yet. For Windows there's Opia for Windows.

Can it open my Lightroom catalog?

No. Lens has its own catalog format. It reads XMP sidecar files, so ratings, keywords and edits saved that way come across.

Will it change my original files?

No. Edits are stored separately, and Imagine, Motion and Folio save new files. Your originals are only overwritten if you choose to save over them.

What can't it do?

Photo editing is 8-bit, Imagine has no PSD support or layer masks, Motion has no speed changes or keyframes, and Folio can't rewrite existing PDF text or read scans. The README lists every limit.

Free, for Macs with Apple silicon or Intel.

Download for Apple silicon Download for Intel Macs

Not sure which? Apple menu › About This Mac says "Chip: Apple M…" or "Processor: …Intel…". Open the .dmg and drag Opia to Applications. Opia isn't notarised by Apple, so the first time macOS stops it: click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, click Open Anyway, then open Opia again. After that, Help › Check for Updates keeps it current. On Windows? Opia for Windows. On a phone? Opia for Android.
